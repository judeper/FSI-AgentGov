# Control 2.7: Vendor and Third-Party Risk Management

**Control ID:** 2.7<br>
**Pillar:** Management<br>
**Regulatory Reference:** GLBA Section 501(b), SOX Section 404, FINRA Rule 4511, FINRA Rule 3110, FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09, SEC Rule 17a-4(f), OCC Bulletin 2026-13 (formerly OCC Bulletin 2011-12) / Fed SR 26-2 (formerly SR 11-7), OCC Bulletin 2013-29, Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships (FRB/FDIC/OCC, June 2023), OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025)<br>
**Last UI Verified:** May 2026<br>
**Governance Levels:** Baseline / Recommended / Regulated

**Last Verified:** 2026-11-06 — technical documentation only; no tenant UI, screenshots, API execution, or compliance reassessment. Historical **Last UI Verified** is unchanged.

---

## Objective

Establish a comprehensive framework for identifying, assessing, and managing risks associated with third-party vendors and connectors used by AI agents. Vendor risk management addresses the unique risks introduced by Power Platform connectors, custom APIs, external AI services, and dynamic tool loading.

    - **OCC Bulletin 2013-29 / Bulletin 2020-10 FAQs:** Third-party risk management expectations for national banks, including risk-tiered due diligence and ongoing monitoring

    - **FINRA Rule 4511 / SEC Rule 17a-4(f):** Books-and-records vendors (archiving, WORM) supporting AI-generated content must meet electronic recordkeeping requirements; recent amendments (effective May 2023) accept audit-trail alternatives but still require demonstrable immutability
    - **FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 / Rule 3110:** Reinforces that AI-generated communications are subject to existing recordkeeping and supervision rules — vendor capability gaps become firm-level deficiencies
    - **OWASP LLM Top 10 (LLM03 Supply Chain, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM07 Insecure Plugin Design):** Identify supply-chain, plugin, and tool-loading risks unique to LLM-backed agents

## Control Description

AI agents frequently connect to external services, APIs, and data sources that may introduce security vulnerabilities, compliance gaps, or operational dependencies:

| Capability | Description | FSI Application |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Connector Inventory** | Catalog all third-party connectors | Regulatory examination readiness |
| **Vendor Risk Assessment** | Evaluate vendor security and compliance | Interagency Guidance requirements |
| **Contract Management** | Security clauses and SLAs | Contractual protections |
| **Dynamic Tool Governance** | Control runtime plugin loading | OWASP supply chain risk |
| **Ongoing Monitoring** | Continuous vendor oversight | Emerging risk detection |

### Connector Risk Categories

| Category | Examples | Risk Level | Assessment Frequency |
|----------|----------|------------|---------------------|
| **Microsoft First-Party** | Dataverse, SharePoint, Teams | Low | Annual |
| **Certified Third-Party** | Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow | Medium | Semi-annual |
| **Independent Publisher** | Community connectors | High | Quarterly |
| **Custom Connectors** | Organization-built APIs | Medium-High | Quarterly |
| **External AI Services** | OpenAI, third-party LLMs | High | Quarterly |

### FSI-Specific Vendor Categories

Financial services organizations typically integrate with specialized vendors. Include these categories in your vendor risk assessment:

| Vendor Category | Example Vendors | Key Assessment Areas |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Archiving/WORM Storage** | Smarsh, Global Relay, Proofpoint Archive, Bloomberg Vault | SEC 17a-4 attestation, immutability certification, AI content support |
| **Communication Compliance** | Theta Lake, NICE Actimize, Behavox | AI interaction capture, FINRA Rule 4511 / RN 24-09 recordkeeping support, review workflow |
| **Identity Verification** | Jumio, Onfido, IDology | Synthetic identity detection, deepfake detection, KYC support |
| **LLM/AI Providers** | Azure OpenAI, Amazon Bedrock, Anthropic | Data residency, training data policy, SOC 2 certification |
| **Copilot Plugins** | Third-party M365 Copilot plugins | Data access scope, permission requirements, publisher verification |
| **MCP Tool Providers** | Custom MCP server implementations | API security, data handling, logging capabilities |

!!! note "Model provider and route-specific evidence"
    Copilot Studio supports primary model selection on **Overview → Model**. Model names, GA/preview/experimental status, regional availability and retirements vary; use the [current model matrix](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/authoring-select-agent-model), not a blanket “ChatGPT-5 and Claude are GA” assertion. Preview/experimental models are not intended for production.

    Anthropic is a **Microsoft subprocessor**, not a Microsoft first-party provider. Microsoft Product Terms/DPA cover applicable Claude routes, but **Anthropic models with Data Retention** require separate enablement and Anthropic terms, with Anthropic acting as an independent processor. Verify the selected route, access, processing and retention commitments ([Anthropic guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/implement/connect-to-ai-subprocessor)). External provider access in Microsoft 365 and PPAC environment/group controls are separate ([external model settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/allow-llm-generative-responses)). Native selection does not make every provider's terms, certification or residency coverage identical.

> **MCP clarification:** MCP is an open protocol supported natively by Copilot Studio, including prebuilt Microsoft MCP connectors and custom/third-party servers ([MCP integration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/mcp-add-components-to-agent)). Classify risk by publisher, hosting operator, downstream service and data, not protocol alone. ACP supports MCP server-level blocking, not individual MCP tool controls; custom/HTTP/virtual connector gaps still need classic DLP ([ACP limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/advanced-connector-policies)).

**Vendor Assessment Checklist for AI Integrations:**

1. Does the vendor support AI-generated content archiving per FINRA Rule 4511 / RN 24-09 recordkeeping requirements?
2. Can the vendor distinguish AI-generated vs human-generated content in records?
3. Does the vendor provide immutable storage (WORM) for AI interactions?
4. What is the vendor's data residency and AI training policy?
5. Does the vendor have SOC 2 Type II attestation covering AI services?

## Key Configuration Points

For registered Microsoft 365 plugins/MCP servers, distinguish publisher-category and individual-plugin availability from server **Block / Unblock** under **Agents → Tools → Registry**. Agent 365 individual-tool controls are rolling out for supported server types, not universal ACP granularity. Blocking a plugin leaves its MCP server available to other plugins unless the server is separately blocked ([current plugin/MCP controls](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-agent-365/admin/manage-plugins-skills-mcp-servers)). Verify tenant support and the actual hosting route.

- Maintain complete inventory of all third-party connectors with risk classifications
- Apply the **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management (FRB/FDIC/OCC, June 2023)** lifecycle stages to every Zone 2/3 AI / connector vendor: planning → due diligence and third-party selection → contract negotiation → ongoing monitoring → termination
- Apply **OCC Bulletin 2013-29** risk-tiered due-diligence depth (and the **OCC Bulletin 2020-10** FAQs) to scope vendor assessment effort to the criticality of the connector or AI service; document the tiering decision per vendor
- Require SOC 2 Type II (or equivalent) for Zone 3 vendors
- Implement AI-specific contract clauses (model change notification, no training on customer data)
- Apply organizational default-deny requirements through supported connector/server policies, agent tool selections and maker approvals; no universal Copilot Studio marketplace or tool auto-update switch is assumed. For MCP, turn **Allow all** off and approve only needed tools; newly added tools then default off.
- Map transitive data exposure for tool chains invoking multiple third parties
- Establish review cadence (monthly usage, quarterly performance, annual security) consistent with the Interagency 2023 Guidance ongoing-monitoring expectations
- Document exit plans for critical vendor relationships, aligned to the Interagency 2023 Guidance termination-stage expectations

## Zone-Specific Requirements


!!! info "Zone cross-reference"
    **Baseline** = Zone 1 (Personal Productivity) · **Recommended** = Zone 2 (Team Collaboration) · **Regulated** = Zone 3 (Enterprise Managed). Zone requirements below correspond to the Governance Level in the control header.

| Zone | Requirement | Rationale |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Zone 1** (Personal) | Maintain a connector inventory (refreshed at least annually); rely on Microsoft and verified-publisher connectors only; standard contract terms acceptable; no custom or independent-publisher connectors without exception approval | Personal-productivity scope; limited blast radius but still subject to firm-wide DLP and recordkeeping obligations |
| **Zone 2** (Team) | Formal vendor questionnaire on intake; SOC 2 Type II (or ISO 27001 + bridge letter) recommended for non-Microsoft vendors; quarterly usage and risk review; documented business owner per connector; AI-specific contract clauses for any LLM-backed integration | Shared agents extend data exposure across teams; FINRA 3110 supervisory expectations apply |
| **Zone 3** (Enterprise) | Comprehensive risk-tiered due diligence aligned to Interagency 2023 Guidance; SOC 2 Type II required (SOC 1 where financially material); continuous monitoring (telemetry, security advisories, financial health); contractual audit rights; documented and tested exit/contingency plan; board- or committee-level reporting; transitive data-flow map maintained | Customer-facing or financially material; subject to OCC/Fed/SEC examination; vendor failure could constitute a recordkeeping or safeguards breach |

## Verification Criteria

Confirm control effectiveness by verifying:

1. Connector inventory is current (refreshed at least quarterly for Zone 2/3) and includes publisher type, business owner, environments, data classes, and risk tier
2. SOC 2 Type II (or accepted equivalent) on file and within validity window for every Zone 2/3 third-party vendor; gaps tracked with compensating controls and risk-acceptance dates
3. Vendor contracts for AI/LLM providers include the AI-specific clauses (model change notice, no-training-on-customer-data, AI incident notification, audit rights, data residency)
4. Power Platform DLP policies block independent-publisher and unapproved custom connectors in Zone 2/3 environments by default; exception register is maintained and reviewed
5. Transitive data-flow maps exist for every Zone 3 multi-tool agent and identify downstream subprocessors, residency, and recordkeeping coverage
6. Quarterly vendor risk report is delivered to the AI governance committee (and board/risk committee where required); evidence retained per FINRA 4511 / SEC 17a-4 retention schedules
7. Documented exit/contingency plan exists for every Zone 3 critical vendor, with at least one tabletop test in the prior 12 months
