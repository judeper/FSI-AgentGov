# Brief 7 — UX & Adoption (cross-cutting)

{{PROVENANCE}}

## Your task
Review the **overall user experience and adoption** of the site — the holistic,
end-user lens. This is the brief that owns the core problem:

> **The site gets low traffic and is widely perceived as dry technical documentation.
> We want it to be genuinely useful, approachable, and engaging for our audience —
> without dumbing down the substance or overclaiming on compliance.**

You are **not** scoped to one content area; you look across the whole thing as a
first-time and returning user would. You complement Brief 5 (Framework & IA): they own
*structure/navigation*; **you own experience, engagement, value, and "is it worth using".**

## Where it is in the package (your primary inputs are visual)
- **`_rendered-site/`** — open `index.html` and browse the **real site** (look, feel,
  tone, flow). This is your main artifact.
- **`_pdf/`** — visual fidelity for how pages actually present.
- `00-START-HERE.md` (audience + the low-traffic context), `00-SITE-MAP.md` (the shape),
  and the home/getting-started/assessment pages especially.
- Skim the area files as needed for substance.

## Lens for this area (plus the shared rubric)
- **First impression / value proposition** — in the first 10 seconds on the home page,
  is it clear *what this is, who it's for, and why it's worth their time*? What signals
  "dry technical docs," and what would make it feel useful and credible instead?
- **Audience fit** — it's for **busy US-FSI M365 admins / compliance leads**, not
  developers. Does the tone, density, and framing match? Where does it feel author-centric?
- **The adoption funnel** — awareness → first visit → onboarding → first action (assessment
  or one control) → return. Where do users drop off? What's the single highest-leverage
  improvement to engagement?
- **"Useful, not just informative"** — calls-to-action, interactivity, the assessment as a
  hook, downloadable artifacts, "show me what to do next". Is the site a *tool* or a *manual*?
- **User input & feedback** — how do users give feedback / ask questions / signal what they
  need? Is there a loop? (Note any feedback widget / contact paths.)
- **Discoverability / low traffic** — likely causes (entry points, titles/metadata/SEO,
  shareability, social/preview cards, internal vs external discoverability) and concrete fixes.
- **Tone & approachability** — could plain-language intros, examples, visuals, or a clearer
  "start here" reduce the technical-manual feel without losing rigor?

## Deliverable emphasis
In your `RESPONSE-TEMPLATE.md` summary, lead with the **3 changes most likely to move
traffic and engagement**, and be concrete (what to change, where, and why it helps).
Use ID prefix `UX-`. Critique what exists; propose realistic, prioritised improvements.
No repository access needed.
